# Design Target

## About "9.8"

A designer cannot honestly assign a 9.8 BGG rating. That score would require a
large body of independent ratings after publication. The useful target is to
build a prototype capable of earning exceptional repeat play.

## Version 0.3 Thesis

The player should see the war on the table. Four coalition Hosts now occupy and
move through the same map as the Legions. The opposition remains low-upkeep:
every Crisis activates exactly one Host with exactly one order.

## Exceptional-Game Gates

- The map state creates the hardest decision in at least 70% of plays.
- No enemy turn requires more than one marker move and one optional Clash roll.
- Players identify the threatened route before reading explanatory text.
- At least three distinct strategic openings remain viable in Scenario 1.
- Retreat, pursuit, garrison, and political peace all matter across repeat play.
- Losses are legible: the player can name the neglected route or accepted risk.
- The historical situation changes priorities rather than merely changing
  numeric difficulty.
- Median third-play rating reaches 8.5+ in blind testing before any public claim
  of excellence is made.

## What Is Deliberately Absent

- individual enemy-unit movement;
- hidden combat tables;
- supply-line tracing;
- stacking limits;
- terrain modifiers by space;
- separate tactical battle boards; and
- an automa flowchart.

Depth should come from timing, concentration, route control, and political
cost, not from procedural weight.
