# Historical Notes

## Scope

The game focuses on the long northern crisis usually called the Marcomannic
Wars, beginning with frontier incursions in the later 160s and continuing until
Marcus Aurelius's death in 180 CE. Rome fought shifting coalitions rather than
a single unified enemy. The Marcomanni and Quadi were Germanic peoples; the
Iazyges were Sarmatian. Other peoples entered the conflict at different times.

## Campaign Frame

- **161:** Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus become co-emperors.
- **165/166 onward:** epidemic disease, conventionally called the Antonine
  Plague, damages population, finance, and military readiness. Its precise
  pathogen remains debated.
- **166-168:** incursions and negotiations expose pressure along the Danube.
- **168-169:** Marcus and Lucius travel north; Lucius dies in 169.
- **c. 169-171:** a major invasion reaches northern Italy and threatens
  Aquileia. Its exact dating is disputed.
- **172-175:** Roman counteroffensives strike the Marcomanni, Quadi, and Iazyges.
  Ancient accounts associate the Quadi campaign with the "rain miracle."
- **175:** Avidius Cassius rebels in the east, interrupting northern plans.
- **176:** Marcus and Commodus celebrate a triumph.
- **178-180:** renewed campaigning north of the Danube.
- **17 March 180:** Marcus dies during the northern war. Commodus soon makes
  peace rather than continuing his father's apparent annexation plans.

## Politics and Finance

The Senate was not a modern parliament and did not direct imperial strategy.
Its support still mattered as a source of legitimacy, elite cooperation,
provincial administration, and command talent. The game's Senate track
compresses these relationships into one visible pressure.

Ancient and later accounts describe Marcus auctioning imperial valuables during
the war rather than relying only on extraordinary taxation. The game treats
this as a strategic choice, not a sentimental anecdote.

## Marcus and Stoicism

The `Meditations` are private exercises in discipline, judgment, mortality, and
duty, not a battlefield manual. The game therefore avoids turning Stoicism into
magic or a generic morale bonus. **Assent** asks the player to accept an
uncontrolled result and decide what kind of inner resource can be preserved.

## Representation

Roman monuments present the wars as justified imperial victory. The Column of
Marcus Aurelius also depicts burning settlements, captives, displacement, and
violence. This game does not label entire peoples as a faceless "barbarian"
enemy. Host Strength represents coalition size, raids, strategic pressure, and
failed diplomacy. Mercy measures whether force is paired with restraint and
settlement.

## Compression and Uncertainty

The nine-space network is an operational abstraction, not a scale map. Aquileia,
Virunum, Lauriacum, Carnuntum, and Sirmium anchor recognizable Roman routes and
bases; the four enemy spaces compress much larger and shifting territories.
Legions are strength points rather than named full formations. Crisis order
varies so that history creates constraints without becoming a scripted quiz.

The date of the invasion of Italy, details of individual battles, Marcus's
annexation intentions, and the interpretation of the rain miracle remain
debated. Scenario text uses `c.` and conditional wording where appropriate.
