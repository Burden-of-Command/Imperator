# Playtest Protocol

## Record Each Game

1. Scenario, result, score, rounds, and strategy.
2. Final tracks, Legion and Army locations, total Strength, and Momentum.
3. Command cards used for Imperium versus Officium.
4. Battles, Exertions, Assents, negotiations, and Legion losses.
5. Battle doctrines, Coalition surges, Envoys, and devastated/restored bases.
6. Historical Mandate progress and the decision made specifically because of it.
7. The hardest decision and any turn with an obvious answer.
8. Rules questions and setup-to-finish duration.

## Prototype Targets

- First-scenario win rate: 55-70% after one learning play.
- Late-scenario win rate: 30-50%.
- No starting move chosen in more than 70% of games.
- Imperium/Officium use between 35/65 and 65/35.
- At least one meaningful choice between routes or settlements in 80% of games.
- Enemy Design resolves in under 20 seconds normally and under 35 seconds
  during a Coalition Surge.
- Players can predict each Army's next destination without consulting rules.
- Median duration below 50 minutes.
- Fewer than two rulebook lookups after the first game.
- Fewer than 10% of losses described as predetermined.

## Questions

Rate 1-10:

- I understood why I won or lost.
- Moving Legions before battle mattered.
- I could anticipate and influence the next Coalition Surge.
- Each enemy Army demanded a recognizably different response.
- My battle doctrine changed what I was trying to accomplish.
- Devastated settlements changed my priorities without creating busywork.
- Rome and the Senate created real military tradeoffs.
- The game felt restrained and severe rather than busy.
- The scenario evoked a distinct historical crisis.
- I could describe this scenario's strategic problem without naming its events.
- The historical epilogue and legacy felt earned by my decisions.
- I wanted to try another scenario immediately.

Do not treat simulator results as proof of quality. They identify obvious
balance failures; human play must validate tension, clarity, and historical
meaning.
